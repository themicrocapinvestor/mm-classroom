# Module 3: Reading the Annual Report (the 10-K)

The 10-K is the one document in which a US-listed company must tell the whole truth about its year: audited numbers, named risks, and management's own explanation of what happened. It looks frightening and runs to hundreds of pages, but its structure is identical for every company. In this module we open Apple's real 10-K and learn to read it without fear, finishing with a 45-minute skim you can use on any company.

1. [What a 10-K is and why it runs 300 pages](/m03/c01/): the company's full school file, who forces it to exist, and why length is normal.
2. [Item 1 Business: how the company actually makes money](/m03/c02/): what it sells, who buys, who makes it, and when the busy season is.
3. [Item 1A Risk Factors: what keeps management awake](/m03/c03/): the company's own worry list, and how to separate real risks from legal filler.
4. [MD&A: management explains the year in its own words](/m03/c04/): the teacher's remark on the report card, read beside the actual numbers.
5. [The notes: where the bodies are buried (debt, leases, lawsuits, segments)](/m03/c05/): the back pages where the important, unflattering detail hides, plus three five-year checks on whether the profit is real.
6. [The 45-minute skim: a full method from cover to cover](/m03/c06/): a fixed route through any 10-K, ending in a five-line summary that prints the line that moves against you.
