# Item 1 Business: how the company actually makes money

<div class="learn" markdown="1">

**What you will learn**

- What Item 1 of a 10-K contains, and why it is the best first read
- The five questions Item 1 answers about any company
- How Apple actually makes its money, in its own words
- Why you should never assume you know a company's business model

</div>

Imagine your friend's family runs a kirana store. If someone asked "how does this shop earn?", you would not show them the till. You would explain: what they sell, who buys from them, where the stock comes from, which months are busy, and which shop across the road fights them for customers. Item 1 of a 10-K is exactly that page for a company, written by the company itself.

Item 1 is almost all words, no tables. It answers five questions:

- **What does it sell?** The products and services, listed plainly.
- **Who buys, and how does it reach them?** Customers and sales channels.
- **Who actually makes the stuff?** Suppliers and factories.
- **When is the busy season?** Companies call this seasonality.
- **Who does it fight?** Competition, in the company's own assessment.

## Apple's answers, from its 2025 10-K

The very first sentence of Item 1 describes the whole company: "The Company designs, manufactures and markets smartphones, personal computers, tablets, wearables and accessories, and sells a variety of related services." One sentence, entire business.

The detail behind it:

- **Products:** five families. iPhone, Mac computers, iPad tablets, Wearables (Apple Watch, AirPods, Vision Pro), and Home and Accessories (Apple TV, HomePod, cables and cases).
- **Services:** advertising, AppleCare support plans, iCloud storage, the App Store and content subscriptions like Apple Music, and payment services like Apple Pay. This is the quiet giant: services brought in $109 billion in 2025, about ₹9.5 lakh crore at roughly ₹88 to a dollar.
- **How it sells:** 40% of sales through Apple's own stores and website, 60% through partners like mobile carriers and electronics chains.
- **Seasonality:** the October to December quarter is the biggest, because of holiday shopping and new iPhones launching in September.
- **Who makes it:** mostly not Apple. A significant majority of manufacturing is done by outsourcing partners, mainly in China, India, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan and Vietnam.
- **People:** about 166,000 full-time equivalent employees (part-timers counted as fractions).

One detail for Indian readers: in Apple's list of geographic segments, India sits inside "Europe", because Apple groups Europe, India, the Middle East and Africa together. Never assume the map matches your school atlas.

![The start of Item 1 Business in Apple's 10-K]({{ "/assets/shots/edgar-10k-business.png" | relative_url }})

Notice how the filing opens with plain product descriptions, not numbers. The rupee figures and growth rates come later, in Item 7 and the notes.

![Apple's overview page on StockAnalysis with key stats]({{ "/assets/shots/sa-aapl-overview.png" | relative_url }})

A summary site squeezes all of Item 1 into a few stats and a one-line description. Handy, but the filing is the source those summaries are built from.

And a warning from another company: most people think Coca-Cola sells bottles of Coke. Read its Item 1 and you learn it mainly sells concentrated syrup to bottling companies, which make and distribute the actual drinks. Same brand, completely different business. Always read Item 1 before assuming.

<div class="try" markdown="1">

**Try it yourself**

Open Apple's latest 10-K on EDGAR and read the first two pages of Item 1. Write five lines: what it sells, who buys, how it reaches them, the busy season, and who makes the products. Then open the [Apple page on StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/aapl/?ref=MICROCAPMINUTE) and check how much of your five lines shows up there.

</div>

<div class="takeaways" markdown="1">

**Key takeaways**

- Item 1 answers "how does this company make money?" in plain words, before any numbers.
- Look for five things: what it sells, who buys, who makes it, the busy season, and the competition.
- Apple's answer: five hardware families plus fast-growing services, mostly assembled by partners, busiest from October to December.
- Never assume the business model. Coca-Cola mainly sells syrup to bottlers, not bottles to you.

</div>
