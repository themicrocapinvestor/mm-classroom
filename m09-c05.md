# From screen to filing: every candidate gets verified on EDGAR before it earns your time

<div class="learn" markdown="1">

**What you will learn**

- Why screener numbers are secondhand and filings are the source
- What EDGAR is, and the three forms to know first
- A five-step routine that verifies any candidate in about fifteen minutes
- How to search across filings, including our own filings-search page

</div>

A screener's numbers are like a WhatsApp forward: usually right, but you have not seen the original. For US companies the original exists, it is free, and the law says it must exist. Before any name from a screen earns your reading time, you check it against the company's own filings. This chapter is the habit that separates readers from clickers.

## Meet EDGAR

EDGAR is the online filing cabinet of the SEC, the Securities and Exchange Commission, the US market regulator and roughly what SEBI is in India. Every US-listed company must file its reports there, signed, under law. Three forms to know first: the 10-K, the big annual report; the 10-Q, the quarterly update; and the 8-K, sudden news like a boss leaving or a factory closing.

![Apple's filing list on EDGAR]({{ "/assets/shots/edgar-aapl-filings.png" | relative_url }})

Notice the steady stream of forms: this is where every serious number about Apple is born.

## The verification routine

Five steps, about fifteen minutes per company:

1. Find the company on EDGAR.
2. Open its newest 10-K.
3. Check the exact numbers your screen used: revenue, profit, debt.
4. Read Item 1A, the risk factors.
5. Skim Item 7, where the managers explain the year in their own words.

![The table of contents of Apple's 10-K]({{ "/assets/shots/edgar-10k-toc.png" | relative_url }})

Notice how the 10-K is organised into numbered items, so Items 1A and 7 are easy to jump to.

Small differences between a screener and a filing are normal: websites sometimes adjust numbers or add quarters together differently. A big difference is a red flag about the website's data, and a reason to trust only the filing.

And if a company does not file at all, or files late again and again, that silence is also an answer: cross it off and move on. Companies that respect their shareholders tend to respect their filing deadlines too.

## Searching instead of scrolling

EDGAR also has a [full-text search](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/): type a phrase like "supply chain" and it searches millions of filings at once. Our own [filings-search page](https://themicrocapinvestor.github.io/smart-investor-tracker/filings-search.html) does something similar for a curated set, built to be friendly for readers like you.

![The Smart Investor Tracker filings-search page]({{ "/assets/shots/sit-search.png" | relative_url }})

Notice the single query box sitting in front of the filings: type a company or a topic and go straight to the documents.

Yes, this step is slow. That is the point. Anyone can run a screen in two minutes; almost nobody opens the filing. Most of what this classroom teaches lives in that gap.

<div class="try" markdown="1">

**Try it yourself**

Take one name from your Chapter 3 watchlist. Find it on EDGAR (the search box on [sec.gov](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/) finds any company by name; [here is Apple's page](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/browse/?CIK=320193&owner=exclude) as an example). Open its latest 10-K, find the total revenue, and compare it with the revenue shown on the company's StockAnalysis page. Write down whether they match, and by how much.

</div>

<div class="takeaways" markdown="1">

**Key takeaways**

- Screener data is a copy; the filing is the signed original.
- EDGAR is free; learn three forms first: 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K.
- Verify the exact numbers your screen used before giving a company your time.
- Small mismatches are normal arithmetic; big ones are a warning about the data.

</div>

**Read one real thing**

[EDGAR full-text search](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/): type "customer concentration" and skim a few hits. You are reading companies' own sworn words, not anyone's summary of them.
