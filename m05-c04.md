# The numbers spine: ten years in one table

<div class="learn" markdown="1">
**What you will learn**

- Why one year of numbers is an anecdote and ten years is an argument
- The six lines that make a spine, and where each comes from
- How to read Apple's ten year table and find the buyback hidden inside it
- Two beginner traps: stock splits and one off charges
</div>

A report card for one term tells you how the term went. Ten report cards tell you who the student is. So the deep dive builds a spine: one table of about six lines across ten years, and reads the story from that.

The six lines: revenue, net income (profit), earnings per share or EPS (profit divided by the share count), the share count itself, free cash flow (cash left after running and maintaining the business), and net cash (cash and investments minus borrowings). Here is Apple's, built entirely from its 10-K filings on EDGAR. Dollars in billions except EPS; Apple's fiscal year ends in late September.

| Fiscal year | Revenue | Net income | EPS | Shares, bn | Free cash flow | Net cash |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2016 | 215.6 | 45.7 | 2.08 | 22.0 | 53.5 | 150.6 |
| 2017 | 229.2 | 48.4 | 2.30 | 21.0 | 51.8 | 153.2 |
| 2018 | 265.6 | 59.5 | 2.98 | 20.0 | 64.1 | 122.6 |
| 2019 | 260.2 | 55.3 | 2.97 | 18.6 | 58.9 | 97.9 |
| 2020 | 274.5 | 57.4 | 3.28 | 17.5 | 73.4 | 79.4 |
| 2021 | 365.8 | 94.7 | 5.61 | 16.9 | 93.0 | 65.8 |
| 2022 | 394.3 | 99.8 | 6.11 | 16.3 | 111.4 | 49.0 |
| 2023 | 383.3 | 97.0 | 6.13 | 15.8 | 99.6 | 51.0 |
| 2024 | 391.0 | 93.7 | 6.08 | 15.4 | 108.8 | 50.0 |
| 2025 | 416.2 | 112.0 | 7.46 | 15.0 | 98.8 | 33.8 |

Now read it the way the dive does, column by column.

Revenue grew 1.9 times over nine years, about 7.6% a year, and not smoothly: a 33.3% jump in fiscal 2021, a 2.8% fall in 2023. Net income grew 2.5 times, 10.5% a year. EPS grew 3.6 times, 15.2% a year. Stop there, because the gap between 10.5% and 15.2% is the whole lesson.

Look at the shares column. It falls every single year, from 22.0 billion to 15.0 billion, down 31.8%. Apple buys its own shares and retires them, so the same profit is divided among fewer shares each year. Profit grew 10.5% a year; profit *per share* grew 15.2%, and the difference is the buyback, a choice made by the board, not something the market did. When you see a great EPS record, check how much is business growth and how much is a shrinking share count. For Apple: roughly two thirds business, one third arithmetic.

![Apple's cash flow statement history on StockAnalysis.com]({{ "/assets/shots/sa-aapl-cashflow.png" | relative_url }})

Notice the free cash flow line: positive every year, never once needing rescue. Then notice the last column of the spine: net cash fell from $153.2 billion to $33.8 billion. Apple has been handing the vault to shareholders on purpose, over $855 billion of buybacks and dividends in ten years against $813 billion of free cash flow. A shrinking net cash pile next to rising profit is not a company bleeding; here it is a company keeping a promise.

![Apple's balance sheet history on StockAnalysis.com]({{ "/assets/shots/sa-aapl-balancesheet.png" | relative_url }})

Notice the equity line shrinking. That frightens beginners until they learn the cause: buybacks reduce book equity by the full amount paid, so a decade of repurchases hollows it out deliberately.

Two traps to avoid when you build your own spine. **Splits**: Apple split its stock 4 for 1 in 2020, so older filings show pre split share counts and EPS. Our table adjusts the early years (multiply old share counts by 4) or the columns make no sense. **One off charges**: fiscal 2024 profit looks like a fall, but it includes a one time tax charge of $10.2 billion from a European court case. The spine shows the bump; only the filing explains it.

<div class="try" markdown="1">
**Try it yourself**

Copy the last ten years of revenue, net income and free cash flow from [Apple's financials page](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/AAPL/financials/?ref=MICROCAPMINUTE) into a notebook and compute how many times each grew. Do the same three lines for Microsoft. Whose spine grows faster, and whose is smoother? You have just done the core move of every professional deep dive.
</div>

<div class="takeaways" markdown="1">
**Key takeaways**

- Build the spine before any opinion: revenue, profit, EPS, shares, free cash flow, net cash, ten years deep.
- EPS growth minus profit growth reveals the buyback. Apple's 15.2% against 10.5% is a third financial engineering.
- Falling net cash beside rising profit can be a promise being kept, not a problem. Read the cash flow statement before judging.
- Adjust for splits, and never trust a single year's profit until you know what one offs sat inside it.
</div>

**Read one real thing**: [Apple's 10-Q for the quarter ended 27 June 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320193/000032019326000020/aapl-20260627.htm), its most recent quarterly filing. Notice the cover page: it states the exact share count, 14,594,180,000 shares, as of 17 July 2026. That one number is how you catch anyone using a stale share count.
