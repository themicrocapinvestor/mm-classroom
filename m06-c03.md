# Reading rotations: leading, weakening, lagging, improving

<div class="learn">
**What you will learn**

- How to read the trail behind each dot on an RRG
- The clockwise rotation pattern and why it happens
- The difference between daily and weekly views
- How to describe a rotation in one plain sentence
</div>

Think of how seasons rotate in a school year. Summer leads into monsoon, monsoon into autumn, autumn into winter, and winter back into summer. No season lasts forever, and they come in a familiar order. Money in the stock market behaves a bit like that. Investors as a crowd get excited about one part of the market, pour money in, get bored or nervous, and move to the next part. This crowd behaviour is called **sector rotation**: money rotating between sectors like technology, energy, healthcare, and banks. A sector is simply a group of similar companies.

The RRG makes this invisible migration visible.

## The trail is the story

Each dot on an RRG has a short line behind it, called a **tail** or trail. The tail shows where the dot was over the last several periods. The dot is today; the tail is the recent past. When you read an RRG, you mostly read tails, because the direction of travel matters more than the current address.

The classic pattern, and it really is common, is **clockwise**:

1. **Improving** (top left): the sector is still weaker than the market, but its tail points up and right. It is gathering strength.
2. **Leading** (top right): it crosses into beating the market, and the lead grows. This is where a trend is healthiest.
3. **Weakening** (bottom right): still ahead of the market, but the tail bends down. Momentum is leaking out.
4. **Lagging** (bottom left): it falls behind the market. Eventually the tail curves up again, back toward Improving, and the wheel turns.

![RRG of S&P sector ETFs, daily view]({{ "/assets/shots/rrg-sectors-daily.png" | relative_url }})
Notice the little tails on each sector dot: the tails that curl clockwise, from Improving up into Leading, or from Leading down into Weakening, are rotations in progress.

## A worked example, in words

Imagine the weekly sector RRG shows the technology sector fund in the Leading quadrant, but its tail has bent downward for three weeks and is approaching the Weakening border. Meanwhile the energy sector fund sits in Improving with a tail pointing up and right, close to crossing into Leading.

The plain sentence you would write in your notebook: "Technology is still ahead of the market but losing steam; energy is behind the market but gaining fast." That sentence is a complete RRG reading. Notice what it does not say. It does not say buy energy or sell technology. It describes a rotation, the way a weather report describes wind. What you do with it, if anything, comes later and needs more evidence, which is chapter 5's job.

## Daily versus weekly: two speeds of the same movie

StockCharts lets you view RRGs on a **daily** timeframe (each step of the tail is one day) or a **weekly** one (each step is one week). The difference matters:

- **Daily**: fast and twitchy. Dots dart between quadrants on ordinary market noise. Useful for seeing the latest wiggle, dangerous if you treat every wiggle as a trend.
- **Weekly**: slow and calm. A rotation that survives on the weekly chart is more likely to be a real season change, not a cloudy afternoon.

A good habit is to read the weekly first for the big picture, then the daily for detail. If they disagree, trust the slower one while you are learning.

![The same sectors on the weekly RRG]({{ "/assets/shots/rrg-sectors-weekly.png" | relative_url }})
Compare this weekly view with the daily one above: the weekly tails are smoother, and the quadrant positions can be quite different because noise has been ironed out.

<div class="try">
**Try it yourself**

On the StockCharts RRG tool, open the S&P sectors on the weekly setting. Pick one sector in Leading and one in Improving. Write one sentence for each, in the style above: where it is, which way its tail points, and what that means in plain words. Repeat in a week and check whether the tails moved the way you read them.
</div>

<div class="takeaways">
**Key takeaways**

- The dot shows today; the tail shows the journey. Read the tail.
- Rotations usually run clockwise: Improving, Leading, Weakening, Lagging, and back around.
- Sector rotation is crowd money moving between parts of the market; RRG draws that movement.
- Weekly views show truer trends; daily views show more noise.
- A rotation reading is a description, never an instruction.
</div>

**Read one real thing**

Open the live sector RRG on StockCharts.com and hover over any sector's tail to see its weekly positions. Notice how many weeks of history one tail carries, and how rarely a tail moves in a straight line.
