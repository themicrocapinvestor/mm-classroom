Module 3, chapter 5: The notes: where the bodies are buried (debt, leases, lawsuits, segments) <div class="learn" markdown="1"> **What you will learn** - What the notes to the financial statements are, and why professionals read them first - The four notes worth reading on a first pass - What Apple's notes reveal about its debt, leases, lawsuits and regions - How to find any note in seconds with a simple search </div> Old newsroom saying: the bodies are buried on the back page. It means the most important, least flattering facts get hidden in the place nobody reads. In a 10-K, that place is the notes to the financial statements: the numbered explanations that follow the main tables in Item 8. The balance sheet gives you one tidy line. The note attached to that line tells you the real story. Think of a tiffin service bill that just says "₹3,000 due". The back of the bill lists: ₹1,200 for this month, ₹800 carried over from last month, and a ₹1,000 deposit refundable only in 2028. Same total, very different story. The notes are the back of the bill. ## Four notes to read first, shown through Apple's 2025 10-K **Debt.** Apple's balance sheet shows term debt of about $91 billion. The debt note reveals what that actually is: bonds paying interest rates from 0% to 4.85%, with repayment dates stretching from 2025 all the way to 2062. Some of Apple's lenders will be repaid decades from now. On top of this sits $8 billion of commercial paper, meaning very short-term IOUs that come due within months. Total debt: roughly $99 billion, about ₹8.7 lakh crore. **Leases.** A lease is a long-term rental, and years of locked-in rent are a real obligation, almost like debt. Apple's lease note lists $16.8 billion of fixed lease payments ahead, about ₹1.5 lakh crore, covering shops, offices and data centres. **Lawsuits.** The contingencies note covers possible future costs whose outcome is uncertain, mostly court cases. Apple says it books a cost only when a loss is probable and can be estimated. For the live fights, look at Item 3: the European Union is investigating App Store rules under its Digital Markets Act. A company rarely writes "we might lose badly"; it writes the case name. Your job is to notice the name. **Segments.** A segment is a slice of the business the company reports separately. Apple reports five geographic slices, and the segment note shows Americas sales up 7% to $178 billion while Greater China fell 4% to $64 billion. One engine slowing while another speeds up: you only see that in this note. And yes, in Apple's book, India is counted inside "Europe". Honesty first: the notes are dense, and nobody reads all of them on a first pass. These four, debt, leases, contingencies and segments, catch most of what matters. Notice how the balance sheet compresses debt into one or two neat lines. The interest rates, due dates and currencies live only in the note. You do not have to scroll 60 pages. Notice how searching a word like "leases" jumps you straight to the note, inside this filing or across thousands of companies. <div class="try" markdown="1"> **Try it yourself** Open Apple's 10-K and press Ctrl+F (or Cmd+F) for "Leases" inside Item 8.
