# Your first ten minutes on EDGAR: a guided tour exercise

<div class="learn" markdown="1">

**What you will learn**
- A complete first walk across EDGAR, against the clock
- How to move from a company name to a real paragraph in a filing
- Three questions whose answers live only in filings

</div>

This whole module was practice for the next ten minutes. Put a timer on your phone. You need only a browser, a notebook and a pen. Ready? Go.

## Minutes 0 to 2: find the company

Open sec.gov, go to the company filings search, type "Apple" and open APPLE INC. Write down the CIK, the permanent roll number you met in Chapter 4.

![Apple's EDGAR company page]({{ "/assets/shots/edgar-aapl-filings.png" | relative_url }})

Notice how much of the company's life is already on this one page: dates, form types, descriptions.

## Minutes 2 to 4: open the year's biggest document

In the filter box, type "10-K" and open the newest annual report. The first thing you see is the table of contents: fifteen numbered sections called Items, running from "Business" to the signatures at the end.

![Table of contents of Apple's 10-K]({{ "/assets/shots/edgar-10k-toc.png" | relative_url }})

Notice Item 1A, "Risk Factors": that is where you are going next.

## Minutes 4 to 6: read one real paragraph

Jump to Item 1A. This is the list of things Apple itself admits could hurt its business. Read only the first risk, then close the document and write one line about it in your own words. Pause and realise what just happened: you have now read something many adults who own this stock have never read. That is real research, done in six minutes, for free.

## Minutes 6 to 8: search inside everything

Open EDGAR's full-text search, type "App Store", and limit the search to APPLE INC.

![EDGAR full-text search with a query typed]({{ "/assets/shots/edgar-fts.png" | relative_url }})

Notice how often the company must discuss its own app shop: developers, fees, lawsuits. One box, thousands of documents.

## Minutes 8 to 10: do it alone

Pick a company you actually know: Microsoft, Nvidia, Coca-Cola or Tesla. Find its EDGAR page, write down its CIK, and open its newest 10-K. If you managed that without looking back at the steps, the tour worked.

Before you close the notebook, keep three questions in it for every company you study from now on:

1. What is the first risk the company admits to in its 10-K?
2. When did it last file an 8-K, and about what?
3. How many Form 4s have its insiders filed this year?

None of these need a paid tool, a finance degree or an expert uncle. They need ten minutes and the government notebook.

<div class="try" markdown="1">

**Try it yourself**

Tomorrow, repeat the full tour for a second company without looking at the steps. If you get stuck, note exactly where: that stuck point tells you which chapter of this module to reread.

</div>

<div class="takeaways" markdown="1">

**Key takeaways**
- EDGAR skill is a habit, not a talent: name to CIK to filing to paragraph.
- Item 1A of any 10-K is the fastest honest look at what can go wrong with a business.
- Full-text search turns thousands of filings into answers about a single word.
- Ten minutes on EDGAR beats ten hours of tips and rumours.

</div>

**Read one real thing**

[EDGAR full-text search](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/). Type the name of any product you use daily and see which American companies are forced to write about it, and what they admit.
